Research

What I work on

My work sits at the intersection of computational methods, big data, and translational biomedical science. I am building a research program in single-cell and spatial transcriptomics, applied to mucosal immunology in infectious and gastrointestinal disease — alongside the network, genome-graph, and machine-learning methods that underpin it, and the reproducible tooling that makes those analyses portable and repeatable.

Projects

Current research

Four collaborations, spanning mucosal immunology, spatial biology, and epidemic dynamics.

Ongoing · 2026

GutEquity

Ancestry-informed risk stratification for inflammatory bowel disease in African American patients. Peripheral blood mononuclear cells are paired with matched intestinal biopsies, and single-cell and spatial transcriptomics used to map the immune-cell states that the circulating and mucosal compartments share or diverge on — centring an ancestry group historically underrepresented in IBD genomics.

With Dr. Sarah Glover · Tulane University School of Medicine and the Tulane School of Public Health & Tropical Medicine
Ongoing · 2026

Spatial transcriptomics in celiac disease

Analysis of a Xenium spatial transcriptomics cohort, using spatial statistical methods to characterise cell-to-cell distances and cellular behaviour across diseased and non-diseased tissue.

Tulane University School of Medicine
Ongoing · 2025–2026

Epidemic infection-rate forecasting

Predictive systems for epidemic spread and emergent viral strain modelling, including forecasting infection rates from contact-tracing information.

With Dr. Dennis Shasha · New York University
Completed · 2025

WGCNA beta-parameter validation

Evaluating the biological validity of scale-free topology in gene co-expression networks across 27 human tissues, with applications to Alzheimer’s disease and autism research.

With Dr. Naoki Masuda · University at Buffalo
